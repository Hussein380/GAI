SECTION 01
Who We Are — Read This First
Global Alumni Initiatives (GAI) is a registered Community Based Organisation (CBO) based in Kenya. We were
founded by a group of international alumni — professionals now living and working across different countries —
who studied together in Turkey over ten years ago on full scholarships. Having benefited from the generosity of
others, we came together to pay that opportunity forward to the next generation of deserving students in Africa.
Our first project (2024–2025) supported students in Uganda in partnership with the NGO that originally funded our
studies. We are now formally registered and building toward a long-term, sustainable organisation that supports
African students through scholarships, mentorship, skills training, and school infrastructure — funded by a growing
global alumni network.

SECTION 03
Who Will Use the Website
The website needs to serve four distinct audiences. Understanding each one will help you design the right user
journeys and features:
1. Current & prospective alumni members
Professionals in different countries who want to join GAI, contribute to the alumni fund, sign up as mentors, or stay
updated on GAI's work. They need a clear, frictionless way to join and give. They may access the site from anywhere
in the world.
2. Students and education partners in Africa
Students or institutions in Uganda and across Africa who want to learn how to apply for GAI support, understand
eligibility, and track the application process.
3. Donors, foundations, and institutional partners
External organisations — NGOs, foundations, corporate CSR programmes, embassies — who need to quickly assess
GAI's credibility, read our impact reports, and explore partnership opportunities.
4. The general public
Anyone who discovers GAI through social media or word of mouth and wants to understand who we are, what we do,
and how they can get involved or support the mission.

SECTION 04
Website Pages — What We Need
Below are all the pages the website should include, with a description of what goes on each page.
Home Page
The landing page. Should immediately communicate who GAI is, what we do, and why it matters. Must include: a
powerful headline, a brief intro, a call-to-action button (Join / Donate / Learn More), a short impact statistics bar
(students supported, countries, members), and links to key sections. Tone: warm, inspiring, and credible.
About Us
Tells the full GAI story — how it started (Turkey scholarships, 10 years later, paying it forward), our mission and vision,
our core values, and a brief founding timeline. This is where the emotional story lives. Should include the founding
narrative and optionally short profiles of key founding members.
What We Do
Explains GAI's programmes clearly: education access (scholarships/bursaries), mentorship, school infrastructure,
skills training, and partnerships. Each programme should have its own mini-section with an icon, description, and
where possible a student story or outcome.
Our Model — Pay It Forward
A dedicated page explaining how the pay-it-forward alumni funding model works. Use a simple visual diagram: Alumni
contribute → Students are supported → Students graduate → Students become alumni → Alumni give back. This is
what makes GAI unique and needs to be explained clearly and visually for both donors and prospective members.
Join Us / Become a Member
The most important conversion page on the site. Should clearly explain membership categories (founding, full,
associate), what membership involves, how to apply, and what members receive. Must include a membership
application form or link to one. Also explain the different ways members can contribute beyond money — mentorship,
skills, networks, advocacy.
Give / Donate
A dedicated giving page with information on how to donate, the different giving levels, and how funds are used. Must
include bank details or an online giving mechanism (M-Pesa, PayPal, bank transfer etc.). Should also mention
Zakat/Sadaqah eligibility for Muslim donors. Include a transparency note — what percentage goes directly to students.
Our Impact
Showcases results and stories. Should include: impact statistics (students supported, funds raised, countries), annual
impact reports (downloadable PDFs), student stories with photos and quotes (with consent), and a project timeline
showing GAI's growth year by year.
Apply for Support
For students and schools in Africa who want to apply for GAI support. Should explain eligibility criteria clearly, describe
the application process step by step, and include an online application form or a downloadable form with submission
instructions.
Partners & Collaborators
Lists GAI's current and past partners — NGOs, schools, institutions. Includes a section inviting new partnerships with
a simple expression of interest form or contact link.
News & Updates
A simple blog or news section where GAI posts project updates, member spotlights, student stories, event
announcements, and impact milestones. Should be easy for GAI admins to update without developer involvement.
Contact Us
A simple contact form with fields for name, email, subject, and message. Should also include GAI's registered address
in Kenya, email address, and social media links. Optionally: an FAQ section for common questions.

SECTION 05
Key Features & Functionality
Beyond pages, the website needs specific features. They are categorised by priority below.
Online Membership Application Form Must Have
A form where prospective members can apply to join GAI. Fields: name, country, profession, scholarship background,
how they heard about GAI, and preferred contribution type. Submissions should go to a GAI admin email.
Online Donation / Giving Page Must Have
Ability to accept donations online. Should support international payments — PayPal, Stripe, or similar — and local
M-Pesa integration for Kenyan contributors. Should show different giving levels with descriptions of what each amount
funds.
Student Support Application Form Must Have
A form for students or schools applying for GAI support. Fields: applicant details, institution, financial need statement,
academic record, and supporting documents upload. Submissions go to the GAI Programmes Officer.
Impact Statistics Counter Must Have
A visual counter on the home page or impact page showing live or regularly updated numbers: students supported,
members, countries reached, funds raised. Should be easy for admins to update manually without coding.
Downloadable Impact Reports Must Have
A section where GAI can upload and visitors can download annual PDF impact reports. Simple file upload system
accessible to a non-technical GAI admin.
Mentor Sign-Up Feature Must Have
A simple form where GAI alumni can register as mentors — their name, country, professional field, availability, and the
type of mentoring they can offer. Feeds into a GAI-managed mentor database.
Mobile Responsive Design Must Have
The majority of GAI's members and audiences in Africa access the internet via mobile phones. The entire website
must look and work perfectly on smartphones — not just desktops.
Multi-Language Readiness Should Have
The site does not need to be translated immediately, but should be built in a way that allows French and Arabic
language versions to be added later without rebuilding the site. GAI operates across francophone Africa and has
Arabic-speaking members.
Member Login / Portal (basic) Should Have
A simple protected area where registered GAI members can log in to access member-only content — meeting
minutes, financial reports, member directory. Does not need to be complex at launch — a basic password-protected
page is sufficient to start.
News / Blog with Admin CMS Should Have
The GAI team must be able to add news posts, student stories, and updates themselves — without contacting the
developer each time. A simple content management system (WordPress, Webflow, or similar) is strongly preferred.
Social Media Integration Should Have
Links to GAI's social media profiles (LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, X/Twitter). Optionally: embed a social media feed
on the news page.
Email Newsletter Sign-Up Should Have
A simple email capture form — name and email — connected to Mailchimp or a similar free newsletter tool. Allows GAI
to build a mailing list of supporters and interested parties.
WhatsApp Contact Button Nice to Have
A floating WhatsApp button on the site allowing visitors to message GAI directly. Simple to implement and very
effective for an African audience.
Interactive Pay-It-Forward Diagram Nice to Have
An animated or interactive visual on the 'Our Model' page that explains the pay-it-forward cycle visually. Could be a
simple CSS animation or an infographic.
Partner Logo Wall Nice to Have
A section on the Partners page displaying logos of GAI's partner organisations. Easy to update as new partners are
added.

SECTION 06
Design Direction & Tone
The following guidance will help the developer and designer capture the right look and feel for GAI's website.
Please treat this as direction, not rigid rules — the developer's professional judgement on implementation is
welcomed.
Colour
palette
Primary: Deep teal green (#085041 and #1D9E75) — the colour of growth, trust, and Africa's
landscapes. Secondary: warm off-white (#F4F2ED) for backgrounds. Accent: gold/amber
(#C8861A) for highlights and calls to action. Avoid cold blues or corporate greys — GAI is warm,
human, and rooted in community.
Typography Clean, readable sans-serif fonts. Suggested: Inter or Poppins for headings, Source Sans Pro or
Lato for body text. Text must be highly readable on mobile at all sizes. Avoid decorative fonts.
Tone of
voice
Warm, inspiring, and credible — never cold or corporate. The site should feel like it was built by
people who care deeply, not by a large institution. Use 'we' and 'our students' — not 'the
organisation' or 'beneficiaries'.
Imagery Real photography wherever possible — students, African landscapes, classroom settings,
community moments. Avoid stock photos that feel generic or western. If stock imagery is used in
early stages, choose African-context imagery. Photos of actual GAI students (with consent) should
be featured prominently on the Impact and About pages.
Feel &
aesthetic
Modern but not cold. Professional but not bureaucratic. The site should inspire confidence in a
first-time institutional donor while also feeling personal and accessible to a student in rural Uganda
accessing it on a phone. It must work for both audiences.
Logo
placement
The GAI logo (to be provided) should appear in the top-left corner of every page. A favicon version
should also be created for browser tabs.
Note to developer: GAI does not yet have a finalised logo. The developer should either propose a simple wordmark logo as
part of the project scope, or leave a placeholder that can be swapped in once the logo is created. The colour palette above
should guide any provisional branding.
SECTION 07
Technical Requirements & Preferences
Platform
preference
WordPress or Webflow strongly preferred — GAI needs to update content (news, impact reports,
student stories) without developer involvement. If another platform is recommended, please explain
why and confirm that non-technical admins can manage content easily.
Hosting
Developer to recommend a reliable, affordable hosting solution. Should include an SSL certificate
(https) as standard. Uptime reliability is important — the site represents GAI to global donors and
partners.
Domain
Domain name to be registered: www.globalalumniinitiatives.org or .com. GAI will handle domain
registration separately unless developer can include it.
Forms &
data
All form submissions (membership, donations, student applications, mentor sign-ups) must be
delivered reliably to a designated GAI email address. Form data should also be stored in a simple
admin dashboard or spreadsheet.
Payment
integration
The donation page needs online payment capability. Minimum: PayPal. Preferred addition: Stripe
for international cards and M-Pesa for Kenyan contributors. Developer to advise on the simplest
and most cost-effective setup.
Google
Analytics
Basic Google Analytics (GA4) integration from day one — GAI needs to track visitors, popular
pages, and donation page conversions.
GDPR / Data
privacy
The site collects personal data (membership forms, email sign-ups). A basic privacy policy page
and cookie consent banner must be included, as GAI has members in Europe.
Speed &
performance
The site must load quickly on slow mobile connections — many of GAI's African audience
members access the internet on 3G. Images should be optimised. Developer should aim for a
Google PageSpeed score above 80 on mobile.
